Operating Systems Scheduling Theory & Principles

SSTF: Greedy Proximity vs Starvation

SSTF minimizes instantaneous seek stroke by clearing dense clusters. However, requests at distant cylinders can suffer indefinite starvation under heavy continuous load.

SCAN: Monotonic Boundary Sweep

SCAN provides an upper bound on wait time by sweeping continuously from 0 to max cylinder. Prevents starvation with minimal turnaround overhead at boundary tracks.

C-SCAN: Uniform Wait Distribution

By servicing tracks in a single direction and immediately resetting to cylinder 0, C-SCAN provides a strictly uniform waiting distribution for newly arrived requests.

Adaptive: Real-time Dynamic Switching

Dynamically selects optimal scheduling policy according to request spatial spread, clustering, direction bias, and arrival pattern metrics.

📘 Project Methodology & Viva Reference Guide

Adaptive Disk Scheduling using FCFS, SSTF, SCAN, and C-SCAN

1. Project Overview & Objectives

This simulator investigates classical disk-scheduling algorithms (FCFS, SSTF, SCAN, C-SCAN) and develops an Adaptive Scheduler that classifies request spatial patterns and arrival distributions to select the policy offering lower seek distance and superior fairness.

2. Phase 1 — Baseline Classical Algorithms

  • FCFS (First-Come, First-Served): Services requests strictly in arrival order. Zero overhead, fair, but suffers from high seek distance under scattered requests.
  • SSTF (Shortest Seek Time First): Greedily services the closest cylinder from the current head. Minimizes seek distance on clusters, but causes starvation for distant requests.
  • SCAN (Elevator): Moves in one direction servicing requests until the boundary track, then reverses direction. Bounds worst-case waiting time and avoids starvation.
  • C-SCAN (Circular SCAN): Moves in one direction servicing requests; at the boundary, resets to the opposite end without servicing and resumes. Provides uniform waiting time distribution.

3. Phase 2 — Adaptive Workload-Aware Scheduling

The Adaptive Scheduler extracts statistical and spatial features:

  • Spread: Normalized cylinder range and standard deviation across disk geometry.
  • Density: Spatial concentration of requests per cylinder span.
  • Clustering: Coefficient of variation (CV) of sorted differences between requests.
  • Direction Bias: Monotonicity and directional net flow.

Dual AI Engine:
• Rule-Based Engine: Domain-knowledge rules based on queue length, clustering, and direction.
• ML Decision Tree: Trained scikit-learn DecisionTreeClassifier using a dual-objective cost function that balances seek distance and fairness variance.

5. Common Viva Questions & Answers

Q1: Why does SSTF cause starvation?

A: If new requests continuously arrive close to the current head, distant requests wait indefinitely while local requests are perpetually prioritized.

Q2: Why does C-SCAN provide more uniform waiting times than SCAN?

A: In SCAN, tracks near the center are visited twice in quick succession (once in each direction), while boundary tracks wait for a full two-way traversal. C-SCAN eliminates this asymmetry by only servicing in one direction.

Q3: How does the Adaptive Scheduler make decisions?

A: It extracts queue features (spread, clustering, density, direction bias). Clustered workloads trigger SSTF; wide bidirectional spread triggers SCAN or C-SCAN; short monotonic streams trigger FCFS.
